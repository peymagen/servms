Section 0

How to Use This Manual

This manual is for the person delivering SERVMS training. It assumes the SERVMS User Manual is available to every trainee as the reference document, and it does not repeat that manual's field-by-field detail. Where a procedure is fully documented there, this manual points to it and spends its space on how to teach and assess it instead.

Before the first session

PreparationDetail
Training environmentA separate SERVMS instance with its own database. Never train on production — every exercise in this manual creates or alters records, and several are irreversible because SERVMS has no edit screen for vendors or users.
Training accountsOne account per trainee at their real role, plus one Super Admin HQ account for the trainer. At least one account must hold Card Signing Authority for Session 5.
Seed dataAt least two divisions, four stations, two trains, one cluster, six units, three contractors, ten vendors at mixed verification states, and a handful of complaints, inspections, and penalties spread across the workflow states.
Sample filesA RailMadad Excel export, a completed station import template, two PDFs under 5 MB, one PDF over 20 MB (to demonstrate rejection), and a PNG signature image.
RoomOne desktop per trainee at 1366×768 or better, plus a projector. Several screens do not fit comfortably on a laptop.
HandoutsThe permission matrix from User Manual §6.3, the exercise sheets from Section 10 of this manual, and the assessment checklist.

Trainers must know this

Several things trainees will expect do not exist in SERVMS: self-service password reset, global search, an edit screen for vendors or users, delete for any role but Super Admin HQ, and outbound email or SMS notifications. Deactivating a user does not stop them signing in. These are covered deliberately in Session 9 (Section 9) so trainees hear them from you rather than discovering them under pressure. Do not let a trainee leave believing a feature exists that does not.

Section 1

Training Objectives

On completion of the programme, each trainee will be able to do the following within the limits of their own role.

1.1 Universal objectives — all roles

  1. Sign in, recognise their role and division scope, and sign out securely.
  2. Read the Dashboard, apply its filters, and drill through to a listing.
  3. Navigate the sidebar and explain why a colleague's menu differs from theirs.
  4. Search, filter, and page through any listing screen they can reach.
  5. Interpret a permission error and identify who to route the task to.
  6. Use the Notification Centre, and state that SERVMS sends no email or SMS.
  7. Escalate a fault with the exact message, screen, and time.

1.2 Role-specific objectives

RoleOn completion, the trainee can…
Super Admin HQCreate Admin-tier accounts, edit any role's permission matrix, delete records, and audit any action across every division.
HQ AdminManage staff accounts and roles, approve every workflow, manage master data, and export from every module across all divisions.
HQ Sub AdminCreate and edit records across all divisions, approve ID cards where holding signing authority, and export reports.
Divisional AdminRun their division end to end — users, masters, vendors, contractors, cards, complaints, inspections, penalties, audit, and reports.
Divisional Sub AdminCreate and edit within their division, approve ID cards where holding signing authority, and prepare work for Admin approval.
InspectorFile, submit, and correct their own inspections, and register complaints.
Dealing SectionPrepare and submit Action Taken Reports for delegated inspections and complaints, record penalty recovery, and export reports.
Complaint CellRegister, classify, assign, and progress complaints within their division.

Audience

2.1 Prior knowledge assumed

  • Comfortable using a web browser, filling forms, and attaching files.
  • Familiar with their own division's vendor, contract, and complaint procedures.
  • Understands Excel well enough to complete a supplied template — needed only for Track A and the RailMadad import in Session 7.

No technical background is required and none of this programme assumes any.

2.2 Group size and pacing

Eight to twelve trainees per session. Above twelve, exercises requiring a second person — every approval exercise — become hard to supervise. Below six, pair trainees so that both sides of each two-person workflow are practised.

Pairing

SERVMS is built around two-person workflows: password reset, transfer, ID card approval, ATR review, and penalty recovery verification. Seat trainees in pairs from the first session and keep the pairs constant. Pair A raises, Pair B approves; then swap. A trainee who only ever raises never learns what an approver sees.

Section 4

Role-Based Curricula

One curriculum per role. Each states what the trainee signs in as, what their Dashboard shows, which modules they reach, what they may do, what approvals they own, which reports they can produce, and — critically — what they must not attempt.

4.1 Super Admin HQ

TopicTeach
Sign-inStandard sign-in. Emphasise that this account bypasses the permission matrix entirely — it is the break-glass account and should be used sparingly.
DashboardFull HQ view with the division toggle. Demonstrate All vs. a single division vs. HQ (records with no division).
ModulesAll thirteen menu items.
Allowed actionsEverything, including the only Delete permission in the system, and the exclusive right to create or manage Admin-tier accounts.
Approvals ownedEvery approval in every module, plus ID card approval without needing the signing-authority credential.
ReportsAll modules, all exports, full audit access.
RestrictionsNone technically — which is the point. Teach restraint: use a Divisional Admin account for routine work and reserve this one for account provisioning, permission changes, and deletions.

4.2 HQ Admin

TopicTeach
DashboardFull HQ view with the division toggle.
ModulesAll thirteen.
Allowed actionsView, Add, Edit, Approve/Reject, Export across every module. Full authority over Role Management including the permission matrix. Manages complaint and inspection type masters.
Approvals ownedPassword resets, transfers, vendor and contractor status, complaint and inspection ATR decisions and closures, penalty approval, rejection, revocation, and recovery verification.
ReportsAll eight report modules, all exports, audit log view and export.
RestrictionsNo Delete anywhere. Cannot create or edit Admin-tier accounts — those go to Super Admin HQ. Cannot approve an ID card without the Card Signing Authority credential.

4.3 HQ Sub Admin

TopicTeach
DashboardFull HQ view with the division toggle.
ModulesEleven — no Role Management, no Audit Logs.
Allowed actionsView, Add, and Edit across the operational modules; Export on Reports.
Approvals ownedID card approval only, and only with the Card Signing Authority credential. Every other approval belongs to the Admin tier.
ReportsView and export.
RestrictionsNo Delete. No approvals except ID cards. Cannot see Role Management or Audit Logs. Entitled to manage complaint and inspection type masters, but the buttons are hidden — route type-master changes to an Admin (see Section 9.2).

4.4 Divisional Admin

TopicTeach
DashboardNo division toggle. Every figure is locked to their own division. Make this explicit — trainees often look for the toggle they saw on the projector.
ModulesAll thirteen, division-scoped.
Allowed actionsSame as HQ Admin but confined to one division: View, Add, Edit, Approve/Reject, Export, plus full Role Management including the permission matrix.
Approvals ownedEvery approval within their division.
ReportsAll modules and exports, division-scoped. Audit log view and export for their own division's activity.
RestrictionsNo Delete. Cannot create or edit Admin-tier accounts. Records in other divisions return “Record not found” — teach that this means “not yours”, not “does not exist”.

4.5 Divisional Sub Admin

TopicTeach
DashboardLocked to their own division; no toggle.
ModulesEleven, division-scoped — no Role Management, no Audit Logs.
Allowed actionsView, Add, and Edit within their division.
Approvals ownedID card approval only, with the signing-authority credential, and only for vendors in their own division.
ReportsView only — no export permission. This is the one place Divisional Sub Admin is narrower than HQ Sub Admin.
RestrictionsNo Delete. No approvals except ID cards. No exports. Type-master buttons hidden despite entitlement.

4.6 Inspector

TopicTeach
DashboardView only. No toggle.
ModulesNine, almost all read-only: Dashboard, Location Masters, Vendors, Contractors, ID Card / QR, Vendor Renewals, Complaints, Inspections, Penalties, Reports.
Allowed actionsInspections — create, edit, and submit their own. Complaints — register only, no editing. Everything else is read-only.
ScopeThe tightest in the system: an Inspector sees only inspections they filed themselves — not their division's, not their station's. Demonstrate this by having two Inspectors compare lists.
Approvals ownedNone. An Inspector never approves anything.
ReportsView only. No export.
RestrictionsCannot assign inspections, decide ATRs, or close cases. Once an inspection is submitted it leaves their control unless a Sub Admin returns it.

4.7 Dealing Section

TopicTeach
DashboardView only. No toggle.
ModulesTen. Read-only on vendors, contractors, units, locations, and ID cards; Edit on complaints, inspections, and penalties.
Allowed actionsWrite and submit Action Taken Reports for complaints and inspections. Record penalty recovery — a right no other role holds. Upload recovery documents. Export reports.
ScopeSees only inspections currently delegated to them. Emphasise that work appears when it is assigned and disappears when it is reassigned.
Approvals ownedNone. They prepare reports and record recovery; Admin approves, and a separate approver verifies the recovery.
ReportsView and export.
RestrictionsCannot create complaints, inspections, or penalties — only act on existing ones. Cannot approve or close anything. Recovery status can only move forward.

4.8 Complaint Cell

TopicTeach
DashboardLocked to their own division; no toggle.
ModulesSix only — the shortest menu in SERVMS: Dashboard, Location Masters, Vendor Management, Vendor Renewals, Complaints, Reports.
Allowed actionsComplaints — register, edit, assign, reassign, transfer, submit ATRs. Vendors, units, and locations are read-only.
Approvals ownedNone. Complaint closure and ATR decisions belong to the Admin tier.
ReportsView only. No export.
RestrictionsNo access to Contractors, ID Cards, Inspections, Penalties, Users, Roles, or Audit Logs. Cannot close a complaint — teach the hand-off to Admin explicitly, because this is where Complaint Cell work most often stalls.
Section 5

Practical Module Training

Session 1 — Orientation, sign-in, navigation, dashboard

1 h 00 m · All roles

EX–01Sign in and identify your scopeBeginner
Objective

Sign in reliably and recognise, from the screen alone, what the account is allowed to see.

Demonstration
  1. Open SERVMS. Point out that any address redirects to the login page while signed out.
  2. Show the “Internal Intranet Access Only” badge and explain that vendors never log in.
  3. Sign in. Highlight that the field says “Employee ID / Username” but only the username works.
  4. On the Dashboard, point to the role tag at the top right and the user badge at the top of the frame.
Hands-on
  1. Sign in with your training account.
  2. Write down your role tag and count your sidebar items.
  3. Compare with the trainee beside you and note one menu item they have that you do not, or the reverse.
  4. Sign out using the button at the foot of the sidebar.
Expected result

Sign-in succeeds and lands on the Dashboard. The role tag matches the assigned role. Sidebar counts differ between roles — thirteen items for an Admin, six for Complaint Cell. Logout returns to the login screen and the browser Back button does not restore the session.

EX–02Read and filter the DashboardBeginner
Objective

Interpret the five group cards and the Critical Alerts panel, and narrow them with filters.

Demonstration
  1. Walk the five group cards — Registered Units, Registered Contractors, Registered Vendors, Penalty, Pending Action — naming each sub-group.
  2. For HQ trainees, switch the division toggle and show every figure change. Explain that HQ means records with no division at all.
  3. Set a From and To date and show the counts narrow.
  4. Expand a card to reveal its chart and switch between doughnut, pie, and bar.
  5. Walk the seven Critical Alerts cards, explaining what each measures.
Hands-on
  1. Record the total on Registered Vendors and its three sub-group figures.
  2. Apply a Contractor filter. Record the new totals.
  3. Expand the Penalty card and switch to a bar chart.
  4. Clear every filter and confirm the original figures return.
  5. Find the highest non-zero figure in Critical Alerts and name the module it would send you to.
Expected result

Filtered totals are lower than or equal to unfiltered totals. The chart renders and redraws on type change. Clearing filters restores the original figures exactly. The trainee correctly names the destination module for their chosen alert.

EX–03Drill through from a Dashboard figureBeginner
Objective

Use the Dashboard as a work queue, and recognise the one drill-through that does not reproduce its figure.

Demonstration
  1. Press Penalty → Overdue. Show that Penalties opens with recovery-pending and an overdue due-date range already applied.
  2. Return to the Dashboard and press Pending Action → License Fee Overdue. Show that Location & Unit Management opens division-scoped only — the figure is not reproduced, because that screen has no licence-fee filter.
Hands-on
  1. Drill through from Registered Vendors → Active and confirm the Validity filter is pre-set to Active.
  2. Drill through from any License Fee figure and note how many rows appear versus the figure you clicked.
  3. State in one sentence why they differ.
Expected result

The vendor drill-through arrives with filters pre-applied and a row count consistent with the figure. The licence-fee drill-through arrives with only a division filter and a row count that does not match. The trainee explains that the destination screen has no licence-fee filter and that the Dashboard figure is the authority.

EX–04Prove the permission boundaryIntermediate
Objective

Experience both permission failure modes so neither is mistaken for a fault.

Demonstration

Signed in as Complaint Cell, type /penalties into the address bar. The Dashboard reappears immediately — the page never renders. Explain that the browser hides what the role cannot use and the server independently refuses it.

Hands-on
  1. Type the address of a module absent from your sidebar. Record what happens.
  2. Find an action your role lacks — a Delete for anyone but Super Admin HQ — and attempt it. Record the exact message.
  3. Divisional trainees: ask a colleague in another division for one of their record codes, search for it, and record the result.
Expected result

Address entry redirects silently to the Dashboard. A refused action shows “You do not have permission to <action> <module>”. A cross-division search returns nothing, or “Record not found”. The trainee states that this message is deliberately ambiguous between “absent” and “not yours”.

Session 2 — Roles, permissions, and user administration

2 h 00 m · Tracks A & B — reference §6, §7, §8

EX–05Create a staff accountBeginner
Objective

Create a working account with correct role and division, and understand what cannot be changed afterwards.

Demonstration
  1. Open User Management and walk the columns, especially Signing Authority and Last Login.
  2. Press + Add New User. Type an Employee Number and show Username auto-filling until it is edited directly.
  3. State plainly: there is no edit-user screen. Everything on this form except role and division is permanent.
  4. Complete the form and create the user.
Hands-on
  1. Create an Inspector for your own division. Use TRN-<your initials> as the Employee Number.
  2. Set a temporary password of at least six characters and write it down.
  3. Give a Designation and a Designation Grade of Other, then confirm Card Signing Authority stays disabled.
  4. Create the user, then find them with the search box.
  5. Export the filtered list to CSV and open it.
Expected result

The user appears in the list with Status Active, Last Login Never, and Signing Authority —. Card Signing Authority remained disabled throughout. The CSV contains the created user with all ten columns. The trainee states that the name and Employee Number cannot now be corrected on screen.

EX–06Grant Card Signing AuthorityIntermediate
Objective

Understand that ID card approval rests on a personal credential, not a role.

Demonstration
  1. Start a new user with role Inspector. Show Card Signing Authority disabled.
  2. Change the role to Divisional Sub Admin. Still disabled — the grade is wrong.
  3. Set Designation Grade to JS. The control enables.
  4. Set it to Yes; the mandatory Authority Designation field appears. Explain that this exact wording is printed on every card this person signs.
  5. Upload a PNG signature and explain it is applied automatically at approval.
Hands-on
  1. Create a Divisional Sub Admin for your division with grade SS.
  2. Grant Card Signing Authority and enter an Authority Designation naming your division.
  3. Upload the supplied PNG signature.
  4. Try to submit with the Authority Designation blank and record the outcome.
  5. Complete and create. Confirm the list shows Signing Authority Yes.
Expected result

Card Signing Authority stays disabled until role and grade both qualify. Submitting with a blank Authority Designation is refused. The created user shows Signing Authority Yes. The trainee can state the two conditions for eligibility without prompting.

EX–07Two-person password resetIntermediate
Objective

Run the full assisted reset and understand why the temporary password must be captured on sight.

Demonstration

Raise a reset for the user created in EX–05, then approve it from a second account. Read the green banner aloud and stress that this value is displayed once, is never emailed, and cannot be retrieved.

Hands-on — in pairs
  1. Partner A: find your EX–05 user, press Reset Password, record a reason, and submit.
  2. Partner B: open Pending Requests → Password Resets and confirm the request is listed with A named as requester.
  3. Partner B: press Approve and write down the temporary password exactly.
  4. Sign in as the reset user in a private window using that password.
  5. Look for a screen to change the password. Record what you find.
  6. Swap roles and repeat, ending in Reject this time.
Expected result

The request appears in B's queue. Approval reveals a roughly eight-character password in a green banner. The reset user signs in with it successfully. No password-change screen exists — the temporary password becomes permanent. The rejected request disappears from the pending queue and that user's password is unchanged.

Trainer’s note

Step 5 is the point of the exercise. Trainees must leave knowing there is no self-service password change, so they do not tell a colleague to “go and change it”.

EX–08Edit a permission matrix and observe the effectAdvanced
Objective

Change a role's permissions safely and see the change reach a signed-in user.

Demonstration
  1. Open Role Management and select a role. The matrix loads below the table.
  2. Walk the 14 modules against the six action columns.
  3. Press Reset to Default and explain that it discards unsaved ticks — it does not restore shipped defaults. There is no way back once saved.
  4. Untick a View box, save, and show the module vanish from a colleague's sidebar without them signing out.
Hands-on — in pairs
  1. Create a role called TRAINING_<initials> with a description.
  2. Select it and note that every box is unticked.
  3. Grant View on Dashboard and Complaints, plus Add on Complaints. Save.
  4. Create a user on that role and have your partner sign in as them.
  5. Partner: count the sidebar items and try to register a complaint.
  6. Owner: grant Edit on Complaints and save. Partner: refresh and confirm the edit action appears.
  7. Owner: revoke View on Complaints and save. Partner: report what happens.
Expected result

The new role starts with no permissions and its user sees an almost empty sidebar. After the first save, Dashboard and Complaints appear and a complaint can be registered but not edited. After the second, editing works. After the revocation, Complaints disappears from the partner's sidebar without a sign-out.

Trainer’s note

Only ever run this on a role created for training. Emphasise that saving takes effect immediately for every holder of that role — an accidental save on Divisional Admin can lock a whole division out mid-shift.

Session 3 — Location, unit, and master data

1 h 30 m · Tracks A & B — reference §9

EX–09Build the geographyBeginner
Objective

Create a station, a train, and a cluster, and understand which can be edited later.

Demonstration

Walk the four tabs. Explain the hierarchy: divisions hold stations and trains; clusters group several of either; units sit on a station, a train, or a cluster; vendors sit on a unit. Show that a train record captures both directions in one row.

Hands-on
  1. Stations: add TRN<initials> in your division with a name and platform count.
  2. Trains: add a train with both directions completed.
  3. Clusters: create a Station Cluster containing your new station and one existing station.
  4. Edit your station's platform count and save.
  5. Look for an edit action on your cluster and record what you find.
Expected result

All three records are created and appear in their tabs. The station edit saves. The Clusters tab offers only Remove — there is no edit, so a cluster must be removed and recreated to change it.

EX–10Create a unitIntermediate
Objective

Create the unit that a contract, a vendor, a complaint, and a penalty will all attach to.

Demonstration

Add a unit. Change Location Type between Station, Train, and Cluster and show the third field switch each time. Explain Contract Type: Earning means the contractor pays the railway; Expenditure means the railway pays the contractor.

Hands-on
  1. Create a station unit on your EX–09 station, Contract Type Earning, named Stall <initials>.
  2. Set an Annual License Fee and attach the supplied sketch.
  3. Leave the Contractor unassigned — it will be linked in Session 4.
  4. Create a second unit with Location Type Cluster against your EX–09 cluster.
  5. Filter the Units tab by Contract Type Earning and confirm both appear.
  6. Deactivate the second unit and note the button's exact label.
Expected result

Both units are created and visible under the Earning filter. The deactivate button reads “Deactivate unit”, not Delete. The trainee explains that deactivation preserves the unit's history in complaints, inspections, and penalties.

EX–11Bulk-import stationsIntermediate
Objective

Run the only master-data import SERVMS offers, and read its error report.

Demonstration

Open Reports → Import Management, select Stations, and download the template. Point out that the button itself names the four required columns. Note that Stations is the only importable master.

Hands-on
  1. Download the Stations template.
  2. Add three rows: one valid, one duplicating an existing station code, one with a Division name that does not exist.
  3. Upload and record the four summary figures.
  4. Read the Error Report and note the row number and reason for the failure.
  5. Confirm the entry now appears in Import History.
Expected result

Total Records 3, Imported 1, Duplicates 1, Failed 1. The Error Report names the row and its reason. The valid station appears in Location Masters; the duplicate is skipped without overwriting; the invalid row imports nothing. Import History records the file, the user, and the counts.

Session 4 — Contractor and vendor lifecycle

2 h 00 m · Tracks A & B — reference §10, §11

EX–12Register a contractor and assign a unitBeginner
Objective

Register a contractor correctly first time, knowing which fields become permanent.

Demonstration
  1. Walk the Master Contractor Listing and its status values.
  2. Open the registration form and note that codes are generated automatically as CON-001.
  3. Point out the PAN and GSTIN length rules — exactly 10 and exactly 15.
  4. State plainly: the edit form is narrower than this one. Company Name, PAN, GSTIN, Registered Address, and Division cannot be changed later.
Hands-on
  1. Register <Initials> Catering Services in your division with a full address, a 10-character PAN, a 15-character GSTIN, a licence validity six months ahead, and an annual contract value.
  2. Try a 9-character PAN first and record the message.
  3. Complete and register.
  4. Open the contractor and assign your EX–10 unit.
  5. Confirm the unit count updates, then unassign and re-assign it.
Expected result

The 9-character PAN is refused with “PAN must be exactly 10 characters”. Registration succeeds with an auto-generated code. The unit appears under the contractor and the unit count updates. Unassigning returns the unit to the unassigned pool without deleting it.

EX–13Onboard a vendor through the five-step wizardIntermediate
Objective

Complete a vendor onboarding, understanding that most of what is entered can never be corrected afterwards.

Demonstration
  1. Open the wizard and walk the five steps: Personal Details, Deployment, Verification, Documents, Submit.
  2. Show that Station and Unit become required only when the chosen division actually has them — the labels change to “(optional)” when it does not.
  3. At Step 3, stress that both certificate numbers are mandatory while the certificate files are not.
  4. Stress the Valid Till dates: an ID card's expiry is copied from one of them, and a vendor with neither cannot be issued a card at all.
  5. State plainly: there is no edit-vendor screen. Only status, the two verifications, and unit or contractor transfers can be changed after this wizard.
Hands-on
  1. Step 1: full name, father's name, date of birth, a 12-digit Aadhaar, a blood group, mobile, and address. Attach the supplied photograph and signature.
  2. Try an 11-digit Aadhaar first and record the message.
  3. Step 2: vendor type Station Stall, your division, your EX–09 station, your EX–12 contractor, your EX–10 unit, and a one-year validity range.
  4. Step 3: complete both verifications as Verified, with numbers, issue dates, and Valid Till dates one year ahead. Try a Valid Till before the Issue Date and record the message.
  5. Step 4: attach the supplied PDF as the Aadhaar document.
  6. Step 5: review and submit. Record the generated vendor code.
Expected result

The 11-digit Aadhaar is refused with “Aadhaar number must be exactly 12 digits”. The reversed dates are refused with “Valid till date cannot be before the issue date”. Submission succeeds with a code in the form V-2026-1234. The vendor appears in the listing with both verifications green. The trainee can name the four things that remain changeable afterwards.

EX–14Update verification and manage documentsIntermediate
Objective

Maintain a vendor's compliance record over time and handle document uploads correctly.

Demonstration

Open a vendor and walk the two verification cards. Press Update Verification and complete the form. Then open the documents folder and show upload, view, download, and replace — noting there is no delete for vendor documents.

Hands-on
  1. Open your EX–13 vendor and press Update Verification on the Medical card.
  2. Clear the certificate number and try to save. Record the message.
  3. Restore the number, set the status to Pending, add remarks, attach a replacement PDF, and save.
  4. Confirm the listing's Verification column turns amber for Medical.
  5. Open the documents folder and upload the Police Verification document.
  6. Try to upload the supplied 20 MB+ file and record the message.
  7. Press View on a PDF and confirm it previews inline.
Expected result

A blank certificate number is refused. The saved change turns the listing's Medical indicator amber. The oversized file is refused with a size message. PDFs preview inline. There is no delete action on a vendor document — replacing is the only way to change one.

EX–15Transfer and blacklist a vendorAdvanced
Objective

Move a vendor between units and contractors, and apply the most consequential status change in the module.

Demonstration

Open a vendor's detail view and show the Transfer panel — two independent transfers, unit and contractor. Then show the Status dropdown, available only with Approve/Reject permission, and the confirmation prompt that Blacklisted raises.

Hands-on
  1. Transfer your EX–13 vendor to a different unit. Confirm the Unit column changes in the listing.
  2. Transfer them to a different contractor. Confirm the Contractor Entity column changes.
  3. Set the status to Blacklisted and read the confirmation before accepting.
  4. Filter the listing by Blacklisted and confirm your vendor appears.
  5. Set the status back to Active.
  6. Open Audit Logs and find the two transfers and both status changes.
Expected result

Both transfers are reflected immediately in the listing. Blacklisting raises “Blacklist this vendor? This should only be done for serious violations.” The vendor appears under the Blacklisted filter and returns to Active. Four separate audit entries are found, each naming the trainee.

Trainer’s note

Track C trainees can observe step 6 even though they cannot reach Audit Logs — project it. Seeing their own name attached to an action is the most effective argument against account sharing you will make all programme.

Session 5 — ID cards, digital signature, and QR verification

EX–22Impose, approve, recover, and verify a penaltyAdvanced
Objective

Run the complete four-stage money trail and experience each rule that guards it.

Demonstration

Project the three independent state families — approval, recovery, verification — and stress that recovery only opens after approval, and verification only after full recovery. Note that only Dealing Section may record recovery.

Hands-on — in threes
  1. Admin: raise a penalty against your EX–12 contractor. Set Origin to Inspection and link the EX–21 inspection. Amount ₹5,000, a due date thirty days ahead, and a reason. Record the penalty code.

  3. Admin: approve the penalty, entering a Recovery Due Date, Penalty Order Number, and Order Date.
  4. Dealing Section: set recovery to Partially Recovered with amount 0. Record the message.
  5. Dealing Section: set amount ₹2,000 and save.
  6. Dealing Section: try to move recovery back to Pending. Record the message.
  7. Dealing Section: set Recovered with amount ₹2,000. Record the message. Then set ₹5,000 with an MR number, date of receipt, and a recovery-proof upload.

Expected result

Recovery before approval is refused with “Recovery can only be tracked on an approved penalty”. Partial recovery at zero is refused. Moving backward is refused with “Recovery status cannot move backward”. Marking Recovered at ₹2,000 is refused with “Recovery amount must equal the full penalty amount”. 6.1 Four rules that govern every workflow

  1. No stage may be skipped. Deciding an ATR before Sub Admin review is refused; verifying a recovery before full recovery is refused.
  2. Remarks are the record. They are the only free-text explanation of why a decision went the way it did, and they are permanent.
  3. Send back rather than reject when the work is fixable. Rejection ends the case; send-back returns it for correction. Trainees over-use rejection.
  4. Decisions cannot be undone. Only penalties offer any reversal, through revocation, and only from the approved state.

6.2 Group exercise — the hand-off map

Fifteen minutes, whole group. Give each trainee a card naming a role. Read out a scenario and ask the group to arrange themselves in the order the work passes between them.

Non-negotiable

Three items are mandatory for every outcome above “not yet competent”, regardless of role, because getting them wrong causes real harm:

  • Knowing that deactivating a user does not block sign-in, and that a password reset is the way to remove access.
  • Knowing that SERVMS sends no email or SMS, so the Dashboard and Notification Centre must be checked daily.
  • Knowing that a printed ID card carries full Aadhaar and address in its QR, and handling cards accordingly.

Trainer’s Notes

11.1 Points trainees consistently miss

MisconceptionCorrection to make explicitly
“I can fix that later”Not for vendors or users. There is no edit screen. Read back before submitting.
“Deactivating removes their access”It does not. Reset the password instead.
“I’ll get an email when it needs me”You will not. Check the Dashboard and Notification Centre daily.
“My Admin role lets me sign cards”Only the Card Signing Authority credential does.
“Re-issue just prints another copy”It invalidates the current card until re-approved.
“I can see my station’s inspections”An Inspector sees only inspections they filed themselves.
“Reset to Default will undo my mistake”It discards unsaved ticks only. A bad save must be re-entered by hand.
“The export gave me everything”Vendor and Penalty exports stop silently at 2,000 rows.
“Record not found means it was deleted”It usually means it belongs to another division.
“Rejecting the ATR sends it back”It ends the case. Send Back returns it for correction.

11.3 After the session

  1. Reset the training database before the next cohort. Records created during exercises cannot be deleted by most roles and will accumulate.
  2. Deactivate every training account, and reset its password — deactivation alone leaves it usable.
  3. Collect the assessment checklists and record outcomes against each trainee's role.
  4. Pass any newly discovered defect to the CRIS support desk with the four-part escalation from Section 9.2.
  5. Confirm every trainee holds a copy of the SERVMS User Manual before they begin live work.
